# HANDOVER: ShotPuck (for the next agent)

Read this first, then `README.md`. Everything below is the state as of 2026-09-30 (rev 0A with the USB-C side port: flat plate, centre bolt, LIR1254).

## 1. What this is

ShotPuck is a Ø40 × 10.43 mm BLE puck bolted onto the free face of an **Avalon barebow riser weight**: the weight's own 5/16-24 bolt, one size longer, goes through a sleeve in the puck's centre. It counts arrow shots and records the bow's motion around each shot, and reports both to the user's own **Flutter/PowerSync archery scoring app**.

- MCU: u-blox BMD-340-A-R-10 (nRF52840, 1 MB flash / 256 KB RAM, PCB antenna). Until 2026-09-29 the board used the Raytac MDBT50Q-1MV2, swapped because JLCPCB had none in stock (README D14).
- IMU: **ST LSM6DSV32X** (LSM6DSV32XTR, C22461202, since 2026-10-01; accelerometer ±32 g + gyroscope ±2000 dps, ±4000 available). v0.1 used a LIS2DE12 accelerometer and v0.2 the LSM6DSO32 (the LSM6DSO32XTR on the BOM from 2026-09-30). The LSM6DSV32X has the same pinout and land pattern, so only the firmware driver changed.
- Cell: LIR1254 Li-ion coin (plain, no tabs, on spring contacts). v0.2 used a Varta CP1654.
- Charger: MCP73831, via a waterproof (IPX8) side-entry USB-C port, power only.

Deliverable status: **rev 0A design package complete and verified off-device. No hardware has been built yet.**

v0.2 changes from v0.1: LSM6DSO32 IMU (U2, plus C7 for VDDIO), raw 6-axis capture per accepted shot streamed over a new CAPTURE characteristic, **pairing required** with new phones accepted only while the charger is attached, unique identity (`ShotPuck-XXXX` + DIS serial), accepted shots kept in a flash log with a paced background REPLAY (all protocol v2), **OTA updates** (MCUboot + MCUmgr SMP over BLE, signed images, rollback, watchdog) on the **nRF52840 module**, and the whole toolchain moved to **native Windows** (KiCad 10, Zephyr on Windows).

## 3. Hard rules of the codebase (do not break)

1. **`layout.json` is the only source of geometry.**
   - Readers: `mechanical/puck.py` (enclosure and balance) and `hardware/gen_pcb.py` (placement, holes, keep-outs).
   - Change geometry there, then re-run both.
2. **`hardware/design.py` is the only source of the circuit.**
   - `.kicad_sch` and `.kicad_pcb` are **generated**; never hand-edit them in the repo.
   - Flow: edit `design.py` → `gen_sch.py` → `check_netlist.py` → ERC → `gen_pcb.py` → `gen_bom.py` → re-export fab files (README "Build & flash").
3. **`firmware/src/shot_detect.c`, `capture.c`, `evlog.c`, `capcodec.c`/`capdec.c`, `capaim.c`, `capstore*.c`, `capwriter.c`, `recovery.c`, `chg_led.c`, `charge.c`, `lifecount.c`, `capjoin.c`, `led_seq.c`, `wire.c` and `evq.c` are portable and shared by the firmware and the host tests.**
   - Any change must be re-run through `firmware/sim/simulate.py --stress`, which must print every `... TESTS: PASS` line (CAPTURE, EVLOG, CHG LED, RECOVERY, CODEC, CAPSTORE, CAPWRITER, CAPWRITER FAULT, LED SEQ, WIRE, EVQ, CHARGE, EVLOG DELETE, LIFECOUNT, CAPJOIN) and `RESULT: PASS` before shipping.
   - Keep new logic that can be portable in this form (no Zephyr includes, flash/BLE behind small interfaces) so it can be host-tested.
4. **Wire protocol lives in `firmware/src/protocol.h`** and is documented in `docs/PROTOCOL.md`.
   - Keep the two in sync (packet layouts are also pinned by `test_capture.c`).
   - Bump `PROTO_VERSION` on any layout change, because the Flutter app parses these bytes. It is **3** now (firmware 0.3.0: CONFIG `capture_mode`; 19-byte v2 CONFIG writes still accepted).
5. `puck.py` and `gen_pcb.py` share only `layout.json` now (the trim pin and its `trim.json` are gone).
6. **Placement before routing:** after any placement or circuit change, run `gen_pcb.py --no-route` (placement DRC), then `global_route.py` (overflow + hotspots; the split-tube board that routed cleanly scored 8.3, boards that failed scored 8.5–9.2, and parts sitting in a hotspot band showed up there), then `gen_pcb.py --quick` (one Freerouting attempt, ~5 min). Only a placement that passes the trial gets the full run.
7. `gen_pcb.py` must end with **0 DRC violations and 0 unconnected pads**. Freerouting is not deterministic; the script already retries up to 4 times, so re-run it if it still fails.

## 6. Key design facts (easy to get wrong)

- **Power:** cell → U1 **VCCH** (BMD-340 pad 65 = nRF52840 VDDH, high-voltage mode). REG0 drives **VCC = VDD = 3.0 V** (pad 17); firmware writes UICR REGOUT0 on first boot and resets once. VDD powers the LSM6DSV32X (VDD and VDDIO), LED and SWD VCC, well under u-blox's 5 mA limit for external loads in high-voltage mode.
- **Battery voltage:** read via SAADC `NRF_SAADC_VDDHDIV5`. The define needs `#include <zephyr/dt-bindings/adc/nrf-saadc-v3.h>`.
- **Charge inhibit logic:**
  - PROG → R1 47k (21 mA for the LIR1254) → Q1A. Q1A's gate is pulled to VIN by R2 (1M).
  - Q1B (gate = CHG_INH on P0.11, R3 100k pull-down) pulls Q1A's gate low, which floats PROG and disables charging. While CHG_INH is high it draws 30 µA through R3, so since 0.3.12 the firmware drives it only while VBUS is present.
  - **Default = charging allowed** (deliberate, so a flat cell can recover).
- **Charge status:** STAT → D2 BAT54J → P0.15. The GPIO pull-up is enabled **only while VBUS is detected**, which avoids standby leakage.
- **Pins:**

  | Signal | nRF pin | BMD-340 pad |
  |---|---|---|
  | I2C SCL | P0.07 | 23 |
  | I2C SDA | P0.05 | 21 |
  | ACCEL_INT1 (IMU INT1) | P0.03 | 19 |
  | CHG_STAT | P0.15 | 33 |
  | CHG_INH | P0.11 | 27 |
  | LED | P0.13 | 31 |
  | SWD | SWDIO / SWCLK | 44 / 43 |
  | VBUS (charger present, System-OFF wake), via R9 1k + C9 100n (net VBUS_SNS) | VBUS | 66 |
  | Supply in / out | VCCH / VCC | 65 / 17 |
  | GND | – | 1–5, 16, 18, 29, 30, 45–47, 55 |

  - LSM6DSV32X is at I2C address **0x6A** (SA0 = GND), CS = VDD (I2C mode), SDx/SCx = GND (allowed with the analog hub and Qvar off, which they are), INT2/OCS_Aux/SDO_Aux unconnected. KiCad symbol: `Sensor_Motion:LSM6DSL` (same pinout), footprint `LGA-14_3x2.5mm_P0.5mm_LayoutBorder3x4y`.
  - BMD-340 pin choice: the module's second pad column (17–29) escapes through the gaps of the outer column (61–68), so VDD, ACC_INT1, SDA, SCL and CHG_INH leave its left end straight towards the IMU and the charger. ACC_INT1 is the only signal on a low-frequency-only pin (P0.03, fine for an interrupt). P0.09/P0.10 (NFC) and the USB pads 67/68 are not used. LED (pad 31) and CHG_STAT (pad 33) sit on the bottom row: the LED drops straight to R4, CHG_STAT goes through a locked via to B.Cu.
  - `design.py`, the board DTS and the pinctrl `.dtsi` must change together. The I2C pull-ups are the nRF's internal ones (~13 kΩ, 400 kHz): there are no external pull-up pads.
  - Footprint and symbol are generated by `hardware/bmd340.py` from data sheet UBX-19033353 R13 (Figure 12 land pads, Table 2 pinout). Its pad table matched two independent open-source footprints of boards that were built (OSHWA 2020 badge, DEF CON 27 badge) to 0.000 mm, and its 68 pin names were compared programmatically with the data sheet table. The 3D model is u-blox's own STEP (`kicad/ShotPuck.3dshapes/u-blox_BMD-340.step`, corner origin, long axis +Y, antenna at +Y), rotated −90° (KiCad turns a positive Z rotation clockwise; +90° put it 15 mm off and backwards) and offset (7.5, −5.1) to the body centre. Checked in a STEP export of the board: body at x −6.5…8.5, y 5.4…15.6, shield can ending at x 3.88 so the bare antenna sits over the keep-out, and all 68 model pads on the copper pads (0.000 mm).
- **IMU configuration** (register values checked against datasheet DS13511 Rev 2 and ST's `lsm6dsv32x_reg.h` in `C:\zp\modules\hal\st`):
  - Init: software reset, BDU + IF_INC, **I²C anti-spike filter always on** (IF_CFG.ASF_CTRL = 1: the part has no separate I3C disable, and IF_CFG survives the software reset), accelerometer ±32 g (CTRL8 = 0x07; bit 2 must stay 1) and gyroscope ±2000 dps (CTRL6 = 0x04), set once. INT1 is a push-pull output driven low by default and is not a boot strap; still never add a pull-up to ACC_INT1.
  - IDLE: gyro off, accel low-power mode 1 (2-sample mean) at 15 Hz (1.875 Hz from 20 min after going IDLE, since 0.3.12; same high-pass setting, cutoff 0.042 Hz), high-pass wake-up since 0.3.10 (TAP_CFG0 SLOPE_FDS, cutoff ODR/45 from CTRL8 HP_LPF2_XL_BW = 011; going idle passes through power-down so CTRL9 XL_FASTSETTL_MODE seeds the filter; 31.25 mg/LSB set in INACTIVITY_DUR, independent of the full scale, so IDLE stays at ±32 g; latched, cleared by reading WAKE_UP_SRC) → INT1. Basic interrupts are enabled in FUNCTIONS_ENABLE.
  - ACTIVE: accel 480 Hz ±32 g (0.976 mg/LSB), gyro 480 Hz ±2000 dps (70 mdps/LSB), both high-performance, FIFO continuous, both batched at 480 Hz, watermark 52 words → INT1. The FIFO holds 1.5 KB uncompressed (~0.23 s at this rate, half the LSM6DSO32's), so the main thread must not block for more than ~0.17 s after the watermark interrupt. The driver reads it word by word (7 bytes each) and pairs each accel word with the latest gyro word.
- **Capture:** ring of 4800 samples (10 s, 57.6 KB). For each **accepted** shot `capwriter` compresses the window (CONFIG `capture_mode`: standard trigger − 480 .. + 240, extended − 2880 .. + 960; starts at the wake if that was later) into `capstore` (since 0.3.11 the aiming part of an extended shot capture, until 1 s before the trigger, as rounded 4-sample means; `capaim.c`) while the post-trigger part arrives, 4 blocks per main-loop pass; the ring leaves ~2 s of margin before the extended window's start is overwritten. A ring reset (wake, FIFO overrun) aborts a capture in progress. Records commit with a CRC; the newest push out the oldest; a RAM index (max 256) finds them by seq. Detector time ↔ sample index uses `cap_index_to_ms` / `cap_ms_to_index` (exact inverses, tested).
- **Clocks and regulators:** 32 kHz from RC (the BMD-340 has no 32.768 kHz crystal). Since 0.3.1 the app runs REG1 (the core supply from VCC) in DC/DC mode through `app.overlay`; MCUboot runs REG1 as LDO. **REG0 has run its DC/DC in MCUboot and in the app on every build since the first commit** (found 2026-11-14): the board's `&reg0 { status = "okay"; }` makes Zephyr set DCDCEN0 at boot (`soc/nordic/nrf52/soc.c`), so every board so far, board 1 included, has run the IMU from a DC/DC-regulated REG0. The board file's comment and the docs said LDO until then. u-blox's data sheet (UBX-19033353 R13, §1.1 and §3.3) says both regulator stages have an LDO and a DC/DC converter with no external components; its table gives 3.3 mA vs 6.3 mA for the CPU and 4.6 vs 9.9 mA for RX with DC/DC on. REG0 DC/DC (−00 modules cannot use it; ours are −10) also feeds the IMU, so its noise is still to be compared with LDO: since 0.3.9 the dev page switches REG0 at run time (CONTROL REG0_MODE, not saved) and records a still capture in each mode (VALIDATION, *Dev page*). REG0's gain is small: about 15–20 % of its current on a full cell, 5–10 % mid-charge and nearly nothing near the 3.3 V cut-off (the LDO's efficiency follows the cell voltage), against REG1's halving of the CPU and radio current.

- **BLE:**

**Why a puck that lost its cell stays dead until USB-C (2026-11-19).** After a cell change, or a contact that bounced (board 1), the puck does not boot until USB-C is plugged in. The cause is the protection IC, not the firmware: TI's BQ2970x datasheet (SLUSBU9I §8.4.1) warns that "when the battery is connected for the first time, the discharging circuit might not be enabled"; the release is to short V– to VSS or to connect the charger. A bouncing contact can also look like a short (the board's capacitors charge with a current spike), which turns the discharge FET off (§8.4.4). It releases by itself only when BAT – V– rises above 1 V while the IC sinks 8–24 µA on V–; the unpowered board draws a bit more, and board 1's dead state measured 0.88 V across the board with the cell at 4.2 V. The nRF has no power in that state, so no firmware change can help. On rev 0B: fix the contacts (item 2), plug in USB-C for a second after fitting a cell (README, *Fabrication & assembly*), or bridge the cell's − contact to a board GND pad with tweezers. A resistor across the discharge FET would let the IC release itself but partly bypasses the over-discharge protection, so it is not recommended without a calculation and a bench test.      - **It does not fix the termination problem.** The board's current still flows through the charger; item 5's power path fixes that.

## 9. Known limitations / risks

- **Thresholds are unvalidated on real bows.** Tuning from logged EVENT data is the #1 on-device task (`docs/VALIDATION.md` §3). Captures now give the raw signals for it.
- **Battery life ≈ 3.5–5 weeks at 2 h/day is an estimate** (45–65 mAh; gyro + accel 0.55 mA whenever ACTIVE; idle ~17 µA estimated). Measure with JP1 cut. Richard chose the single LIR1254 knowing this; do not add gyro duty-cycling unless he asks. The LSM6DSV32X has no ultra-low-power mode (that note dated from the LSM6DSO32); its lowest rate, LPM1 at 1.875 Hz, is used from 20 min after going IDLE since 0.3.12 (about −3.1 µA, estimated).
- **IMU X/Y axes vs the bow are unconfirmed**; +Z is out of the cap. First capture on hardware fixes the mapping (VALIDATION §2).
- **Hard grab off the stand and stand knocks** are the false-positive sources in simulation.
- **Load-bearing but unverified assumptions:** nRF52840 VBUS wake from System OFF; MDBT50Q LFXO (Raytac: not fitted by default; we use the RC oscillator); BLE range through the PC cap next to the aluminium plate; I2C at 400 kHz on the nRF's internal pull-ups (~13 kΩ, estimated 130–200 ns rise on this short bus; there are no pads for external ones).
- **REG1 DC/DC (0.3.1):** enabled for the app only (§6, Clocks and regulators). Unmeasured: check the ACTIVE and idle current with it on and off, and that the radio range and IMU noise do not change (VALIDATION §2). If it misbehaves, delete `app.overlay`; an OTA image with it rolls back by itself if it fails to boot.
- **VBUS detect and charger headroom on a weak supply:** the nRF52840 detects VBUS rising at 3.4–4.3 V and drops it only below 3.0–3.9 V. Detection happens at plug-in, before the MCP73831 starts (5 ms start delay), when D1 (BAT54J, max 320 mV at 1 mA) carries only a few hundred µA: on rev 0A (R9 fed from VIN) a 4.75 V supply brings VBUS to ~4.43 V, 0.13 V above the worst-case threshold. With R9 fed from VIN_RAW (since 2026-10-12) VBUS sits ~24 mV below the supply, ~4.73 V, 0.43 V above it. While charging (21 mA, D1 max ~0.46 V) VIN falls to ~4.29 V, far above the removal threshold. The tighter limit is the charger: MCP73831 accuracy is specified from VDD ≥ 5.2 V, so on a 4.75 V supply the charge may end slightly below 4.20 V. Use a 5.0–5.25 V charger; a lower-drop D1 would add margin.
- **Sustained overvoltage:** D3 and R9/C9 handle ESD and hot-plug spikes, not a 9–12 V adapter; that would need an overvoltage switch on VIN.
- **Stock (JLCPCB assembly, 2026-10-01, USB-C board):** all 21 BOM lines are in stock for 20 boards (5 panels); the rev 0A order assembles 4. There are 10 extended parts; parts cost about $15 per board, most of it U1 ($8.53) and U2 ($3.74). Thinnest: DMN63D8LDW (244), BQ29700 (257), BMD-340 (322); U2 LSM6DSV32XTR C22461202 has 5,864. Earlier swaps: U1 MDBT50Q-1MV2 (0 in stock) → BMD-340-A-R C5456944; D4 → C22470306, a second listing of the same XL-1005SYGC; U2 LSM6DSO32TR (dropped to 39) → LSM6DSO32XTR (177) → LSM6DSV32XTR (lower noise, more stock). LCSC's own shop stock differs and is not what PCBA uses. JLCPCB's search API (`selectSmtComponentList`, POST, JSON body `{"keyword": "<LCSC>"}`) returns live `stockCount`; EasyEDA's `https://easyeda.com/api/products/<LCSC>/components?version=6.4.19.5` returns the footprint JLC places.
- **BMD-340 type:** JLCPCB support confirmed (2026-10-12) that C5456944 is BMD-340-A-R-10; -00 (nRF52840 rev 1) has an unusable high-voltage mode (README D14).
- **Rejected candidates are RAM-only** (128). Accepted shots persist (~900 in flash), captures too (as many as fit in 408 KB, 660 KB with slot 1 since 0.3.8; the count depends on real compression, unmeasured). Captures in slot 1 are lost when a firmware update starts; an image erase from Device Manager also wipes them, and the store's index notices only at the next re-scan (reboot or the next change of area), so GET_CAPTURE can return garbage for those until then.
- **Capture writes erase flash in the main thread** (0.3.0): a 4 KB sector every few KB of compressed data, 85 ms each (longer with a phone connected, when the flash driver fits the erase between radio events). The main loop must read the IMU FIFO within ~0.17 s of the watermark; a longer stall gives a FIFO overrun, which restarts the detector and drops the capture in progress. Unverified on hardware (VALIDATION §2). Fallbacks if it shows up: pre-erase the next sector between shots, or move the writer to a low-priority thread.
- **Unused RAM is switched off (0.3.9).** Once the running image is confirmed, `ota.c` calls `supply_unused_ram_off`, which clears sections 3–5 of RAM8 (0x20028000–0x2003FFFF, the top 96 KB; sections 2–5 up to 0.3.11) in `POWER->RAM[8].POWERCLR`. Two properties of the chip make this easy to get wrong: the setting survives a soft reset and a wake from System OFF (only a power-on, pin, brown-out or watchdog reset clears it), and touching switched-off RAM is a bus fault (Nordic's RAM power-down library documentation). So:
  - MCUboot must stay out of that RAM. It ends at 0x20003C00 and has no heap.
  - **Firmware 0.3.8 and older cannot start with this RAM off:** it keeps its heap in all the RAM above the image and writes the heap's end marker at the very top of RAM during boot. Richard (2026-11-14): a puck that has been upgraded never gets an older version again. The one automatic way back still works: MCUboot can revert an unconfirmed 0.3.9 to 0.3.8, because nothing is switched off before the image is confirmed. If 0.3.8 or older ever has to go onto a puck that ran a confirmed 0.3.9, remove all power (cell and USB) first. J-Link's flash loader runs in the target's RAM and may use more than the low 128 KB, so the NAS re-flash page now halts the CPU and writes `0x3C` to `RAM[8].POWERSET` (`w4 40000984 3C`) before `loadfile`; on a puck whose RAM is on this does nothing. Unverified whether J-Link would fail without it. The RTT control block is at 0x20000000, so RTT's search is not affected.
  - An image whose RAM passes 0x20020000 would bus-fault while clearing `.bss`, before `main`, when it starts after a soft reset from 0.3.9 or newer. `ram_limit.ld` stops the link in that case (release ends at 0x2001CDB0, debug at 0x2001DC30). An image that needs more must switch the RAM on in `soc_reset_hook` (`CONFIG_SOC_RESET_HOOK`) and take those sections out of `UNUSED_RAM_SECTIONS` in `supply.c`.
  - Unmeasured: the saving (about 0.7 µA by the data sheet), and the reset checks in VALIDATION §1 and §2b.
  - **Section 2 holds the ultra mode's 60 Hz history since 0.3.12** (0x20020000, 24 KB of 32 KB; the address is `HISTORY_RAM_ADDR` in `supply.c`, not part of the image). It may still be off when 0.3.12 starts, after a soft reset from a confirmed 0.3.9–0.3.11, so `main` switches it on with `supply_history_ram_on` before `cap_init`, and nothing may touch it earlier; startup's `.bss` clearing never does, because the image ends below it. It is powered only while ACTIVE: `go_active` switches it on before the first sample, `go_idle` switches it off unless the capture writer is still busy (then it stays on until the next `go_idle`). Only `cap_push` (ACTIVE only) and the capture writer touch it, and every capture request comes from an ACTIVE puck; a new reader of the history must keep to the same rule. Datasheet cost about 0.17 µA while ACTIVE, nothing while asleep.
- **Fixed 4 KB heap (0.3.9).** The only heap users are mbedTLS's AES context (280 B) and CMAC context (36 B), one of each per AES-CMAC or AES-ECB operation in pairing, address resolution and the GATT database hash (in the image, `aes_ctx_alloc` and `psa_mac_setup` are the only callers of `calloc`). These run on the Bluetooth threads, a few at once at most, so 4 KB is about twelve times one operation. Anything new that calls `malloc` shares these 4 KB.
- **Settings lookups (0.3.9).** NVS has no index: without `CONFIG_NVS_LOOKUP_CACHE` every read walks back from the newest entry, and a settings save reads every stored name, so the time per save grows with the number of entries since the name was written (up to about 1,700 in the 24 KB partition) and with the number of names (more bonded phones, more names). The cache (512 entries of 4 B) keeps the newest entry per id; two ids that share a cache slot still walk. Do not enable `CONFIG_SETTINGS_NVS_NAME_CACHE` on Zephyr 4.1: a name that is not in the cache always gets a new id, so deleting and re-creating names (Bluetooth does this with a phone's subscription and key entries) uses up the 16,383 ids instead of reusing freed ones.
- **Pairing window:** anyone within range while the charger is attached (at most 60 s after it goes in) can pair. Acceptable because attaching the charger needs the puck in hand; since 0.3.2 unplugging closes the window at once. Since 0.3.4 someone who copies a paired phone's address could, during the window, pair as that phone and replace its bond (Zephyr's warning on `BT_SMP_ALLOW_UNAUTH_OVERWRITE`); that needs the charger plugged into the puck too, and the owner just pairs again.
- **Updates need the charger (0.3.2):** `ota.c` registers an MCUmgr upload-check hook that rejects every upload chunk with SMP rc 6 unless VBUS is present, so an update cannot drain the cell or run with the puck on the bow. Test, confirm and reset are not gated (Zephyr has no rejecting hook for them, and they are harmless without a complete upload: MCUboot checks the signature).
- **Unencrypted links and the pairing marker (0.3.1, review #11):** with one connection allowed, any phone or scanner app that connected and stayed connected without pairing kept the puck from advertising, so the owner's phone could not reach it. The puck now drops a link that is not encrypted 30 s after it connected. archesk 0.3.0-era builds hold the link unencrypted while the user plugs in the charger, so **update archesk before flashing 0.3.1** (handover: archesk `docs/shotpuck-integration.md`, *Firmware 0.3.1: app changes*). The pairing marker lets the app pair as soon as the charger goes in, without a Pair tap. Company ID `0xFFFF` is the SIG's test ID: before a product release, request an assigned company ID and change `SP_ADV_COMPANY_ID` and the app together.
- **IMU health check (0.3.1):** `accel_init` recovers a stuck I2C bus (9 clocks + STOP) and retries once; a puck that boots without the IMU keeps advertising and retries every 5 s (an unconfirmed image still lets the watchdog roll it back). Housekeeping re-initialises the IMU if ACTIVE got no FIFO data for 5 s or the IDLE config (CTRL1, MD1_CFG) reads back wrong. The readback values are unverified on hardware: a mismatch would re-initialise every minute while idle (VALIDATION §2).
- **LED patterns no longer block the main loop (0.3.1):** `led.c` steps every pattern from a kernel timer through the portable `led_seq.c` (a queue of 4 patterns over a steady layer for Charged and the reset pads). In 0.3.0 LED_BLINK blocked for ~1 s, the paused charger pattern 540 ms, the paired blink 240 ms, all longer than the IMU FIFO. The LED report artifact (https://claude.ai/artifact/8JHLgkkEjaV3gsE6Bhvy64) lists every pattern and holds Richard's saved decisions on LED changes (`decisions` collection).
- **BLE transmit buffers (0.3.1):** notifications from the main thread wait for a free ATT buffer (Zephyr allocates them with `K_FOREVER` outside the system work queue). With the default 3, the 2 paced notifications plus COUNT, STATUS and BAS could stall the main loop for up to a connection interval; `CONFIG_BT_ATT_TX_COUNT=8` covers them. Verify on hardware with RTT timing (VALIDATION §2).
- **CONTROL and CONFIG writes (0.3.1):** a CONTROL write that finds the 4-entry command queue full now fails with ATT 0x11 instead of being dropped silently; CONFIG writes reach the main loop through a one-entry message queue (newest wins), so two quick writes can no longer mix into an unvalidated config.
- **OTA is verified off-device only** (build, signatures, key guard). Upgrade, rollback-on-reset and rollback-on-hang must be tested on hardware (VALIDATION §2b). During the MCUboot swap (about 10–20 s) the puck is unavailable.
- **IMU FIFO burst reads (0.3.1):** `accel_read_fifo` reads up to 32 words in one I2C transaction (0.3.0: one per word, 52 per watermark). ST's AN5763 §9.8 (written for the LSM6DSV16X, same FIFO) says the address rolls over from FIFO_DATA_OUT_Z_H back to FIFO_DATA_OUT_TAG for multi-word reads. Unverified on the LSM6DSV32X: check the tags and sample counts on hardware (VALIDATION §2).
- **Crash diagnostics (0.3.1):** `diag.c` overrides `k_sys_fatal_error_handler`: it writes the fatal reason + 1 into GPREGRET0 (upstream Zephyr and our MCUboot do not use it; the boot guard uses GPREGRET2) and reboots at once. At boot `diag_boot()` reads and clears the hwinfo reset cause and the mark, `recovery.c` classifies it, and a crash, watchdog or lockup restart adds 1 to `sp/crashes` and counts for the boot-loop guard. `CONFIG_INIT_STACKS` lets DIAG report the main thread's and the system work queue's lowest free stack (HW stack protection was already on, so an overflow is a fatal error with reason 2).
- **Firmware size (0.3.1):** `prj.conf` turns on link-time optimisation (`CONFIG_LTO`, which needs `ISR_TABLES_LOCAL_DECLARATION`), drops float formatting from printf (`CBPRINTF_FP_SUPPORT=n`, picolibc long-long printf; no code prints floats), turns off controller features the puck does not use (controller privacy and filter accept list: the host resolves bonded phones' random addresses itself; LE Ping; peripheral-initiated feature exchange; min-used-channels), and printk, the boot banner and the SMP echo command. Measured one at a time: LTO −30.2 KB, controller features −7.5 KB, printf −4.9 KB, the rest −0.3 KB; together 208,320 → 168,252 B, debug 239,824 → 196,996 B. Checked in the LTO image: every GATT service, settings handler and connection callback is still registered (same section sizes as before), and the fatal handler is inlined into `z_arm_fatal_error` (GPREGRET0 write, then `sys_reboot`). Side effects: with LTO, Zephyr compiles the controller with the project's size optimisation instead of its usual `-O2` (the controller's optimisation choice depends on `!LTO`), so radio ISR timing changes (VALIDATION §2); stack traces and stepping in a debugger see more inlined code; the build prints `lto-wrapper: warning: using serial compilation`, which is harmless. Not done: dropping the GATT client (−2.8 KB, but then the MTU depends on the phone asking for it), `-Oz` (same image as `-Os` with GCC 12), mbedTLS trimming. Revert any of it by deleting its lines from `prj.conf`.
- **0.3.0 drops the link about 2 s after a bonded phone changes its subscriptions (found 2026-11-09, fixed in 0.3.1).** Seen with archesk 1.0.42 on a Galaxy S21 against board 1: every sync session lost the link (supervision timeout, −55 dBm) about 1 s after the last CCC write, while a session without subscriptions stayed up. Zephyr stores a bonded peer's changed CCCs 1 s after the write (`CONFIG_BT_SETTINGS_DELAYED_STORE_MS`) in `delayed_store` on the system work queue, and that path (`bt_gatt_store_ccc` keeps a 48-entry table, 224 B, then settings, NVS, sector close or garbage collection, and the flash driver waiting for a radio gap) needs 1,048–1,200 B by GCC's per-function stack usage, more than the 1,024 B queue. HW stack protection turns the overflow into a fatal error; 0.3.0 then halts with interrupts off, so the radio stops and the watchdog resets about 10 s later. The store never finishes, so the NVS state that needs the deep path stays and every session repeats it (archesk unsubscribes when it closes, so each session changes the CCCs). 0.3.1 sets `CONFIG_SYSTEM_WORKQUEUE_STACK_SIZE=2048`: the worst cases are now 1,200 B (CCC store), 968 B (GATT DB hash store, which runs after the first boot of an image that changes the GATT table), 680 B (conn param update), 672 B (the 30 s encrypt deadline); DIAG reports the measured headroom. On 0.3.0 pucks: each crash is a watchdog reset, so 3 sessions within a minute of boot trip the boot-loop wipe (settings and pairings erased), and an OTA session subscribes too (SMP notifications) and can be cut the same way: update 0.3.0 pucks by cable (`shotpuck_full.hex`).
- **Sim wake model (0.3.1):** `sd_idle_sample` (used only by the simulator) now matches the IMU: per axis, half the sample-to-sample difference (slope filter, AN5763 §3.6.1), above the threshold rounded up to 31.25 mg steps by the same `sd_wake_ths_lsb()` the firmware writes to WAKE_UP_THS. The real wake is a jump of more than 312.5 mg on one axis between two 15 Hz samples, not 150 mg. The simulated sessions give identical results either way; whether that is sensitive enough when the bow is picked up is a hardware check. **It was not (2026-11-15):** a smooth lift or draw never trips the slope filter (max 122 mg on a 100° lift, 55–146 mg in the 6 s before 37 real shots), so a sleeping puck missed arrows. Since 0.3.10 the model is a first-order high-pass per axis, `alpha = 1 / (1 + 2π/45)`, seeded at the first idle sample like the IMU's fast-settle mode.
- **Material constraint:** PC cap + liquid threadlocker = stress cracking. Use nylon-patch screws only.
- **Mouse-bite tabs:** cut them with flush cutters, never snap (parts sit a few mm from the tabs; bending can crack joints or the 0603 C2), then sand the nubs flush; the board has 0.5 mm radial clearance in the cap cavity. The 3 mm side and bottom tabs and the 2.3 mm top tab have 5–6 bite holes each, within JLC's guide (3–4 mm tabs for 25–50 mm boards, ≥ 5 holes per set). The top tab can't grow much: H1 and the GND via at 80° sit either side. JLC publishes no 5 mm tab minimum.
- **JLCPCB part rotations** come from KiCad orientations plus `JLC_FOOTPRINT_OFFSETS` in `gen_jlc.py` (2026-09-30): each LCSC part's EasyEDA footprint (`https://easyeda.com/api/products/<LCSC>/components?version=6.4.19.5`) was fitted to our footprint by pad number. U3, Q1, Q2 need +270°, D4 +180° (JLC's pad 1 = cathode sits on the other side), D3 +180° (2026-10-01: JLC numbers the SMF5.0CA's pads the other way; bidirectional, so only the preview was wrong), U1 +2.0 mm along its x axis (JLC's origin is the pad-array centre, ours the body centre), J1 0.15 mm; all others match at 0°. With the offsets every JLC pad lands on our same-numbered pad (≤ 0.1 mm; the SOT-363 and SOD-123F land patterns differ by 0.15–0.29 mm in size, not position). Re-fit if a part or footprint changes, and still confirm JLC's placement preview.
- **Thin cap over the port:** 0.80 mm of cap top over the USB-C body, exactly JLC3DP's minimum SLA wall for a ~10 mm feature. The band under the port flat is 0.90 mm since the 1.2 mm board; it was 0.50 mm on the 0.8 mm board, where 0.5 mm is the minimum for ~5 mm features. Everything else is ≥ 0.73 mm on features under 5 mm (ray-cast scan, 2026-09-30); that scan missed the 0.40 mm seal band, which is 0.8 mm since 2026-10-12. Check the roof on the first print. Keep `cap_top` ≥ 1.33 (and `OVERMOLD_CLEAR` ≤ 0.2 if the board ever goes back to 0.8 mm).
- **Hand edits on the board:** J1's notch, the GND via at (−0.41, 16.09), JP2, the silkscreen and the U2/C3/C6/C7 placement (moved inward from the top tab, 2026-10-01) are not generated. Re-running `gen_pcb.py` drops them; redo them by hand or teach `gen_pcb.py` the notch first.
- **Hand edits in the schematic:** `shotpuck.kicad_sch` was saved from KiCad with GND_LOCAL power symbols and 19 wires that `gen_sch.py` does not make (USB-C round). Re-running `gen_sch.py` drops them, so change nets in place (the pin's label) and run `check_netlist.py`.
- **Cell in the PCB notch:** the Kapton under the PCB must cover the plate under the cell (it insulates the + can from the plate). Cut it Ø35.7, so it stays 0.25 mm inside the cap's seal band, with a tab about 6.5 mm wide under the cell, out to the cavity wall inside the band's cell gap. The cell is held only by the + spring, the − strap and the foam pad: shot shocks (8–40 g) must not lift a contact, or the puck browns out. Check for resets over a shooting session (VALIDATION §2). The contacts are not a bought part yet.
- **Bolt head near the antenna:** the steel button head (Ø16.7) sits on the cap top over the sleeve (no washer since 2026-10-12), about 4 mm above the PCB and 2.1 mm above the module, and reach about 8 mm from the axis in plan view, over the module's inner edge. Check BLE range on the bow (VALIDATION §2); a smaller head would help (a standard 5/16 button head is about Ø13.9).
- **Bolt tube:** it carries the whole clamp load (a 5/16 button head at its maximum torque, about 14 N·m, gives ~9 kN, ~400 MPa on the tube end; Richard's normal 3 N·m gives ~2 kN, 80–110 MPa), so it must be cold-drawn tube: annealed 304 yields at ~205 MPa. **The PC cap top is in the clamp path** (Richard's prototype test, D21): 80–110 MPa is at PC's compressive yield, so watch for creep, lost bolt preload and cracks around the bolt hole (VALIDATION §2). It stands 0.5 mm from the cell's + can with no FR-4 between them: keep the Kapton tape on it, all round, since the loose tube can turn. A touch is harmless on its own (the tube, plate and bow are not connected to the circuit).
