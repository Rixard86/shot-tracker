The simulation validates **logic and robustness margins**, not the physics of your bow. The signal model (release shock of 8–40 g, 15–80° rotation into the sling) is an assumption.

## On-device checklist

Do these in order: each step de-risks the next. **Bold** items are blockers.

### 1. Bench, cap off (programmer on the SWD test pads)
- [ ] **PCM wake on first cell:** the BQ29700 may leave its discharge FET off when a cell is first connected (TI SLUSBU9I §8.4.1). If the puck stays dead with a charged cell, attach the charger once; it must then run from the cell after the charger is removed.
- [ ] **First boot:** the LED blinks 200 ms, the puck advertises as ShotPuck, and the UICR REGOUT0 write causes one extra reset.
- [ ] **VDD measures 3.0 V** (REG0 programmed) and the IMU answers (WHO_AM_I 0x6C at I2C 0x6A; no 1 Hz error blinking).
- [ ] **Standby current** with JP1 cut: IDLE and not advertising should be about 14 µA (LSM6DSV32X accelerometer in low-power mode 1 at 15 Hz is about 7 µA, interpolated from the datasheet; BQ29700 4 µA; measure it). ACTIVE at 480 Hz with the gyroscope should be about 0.8–0.9 mA. Advertising at 500 ms should add about 20–26 µA (Nordic's profiler gives 13.3 µC per connectable event; the 10–15 µA expected here before 0.3.12 was probably too low). These set the battery life (README: about 3–4.5 weeks at 2 h/day).
- [ ] **Unused RAM off (0.3.9):** on the same board, IDLE current with a confirmed 0.3.9 is about 0.7 µA below 0.3.8 (the top 128 KB of RAM are switched off once the image is confirmed; the nRF52840 data sheet gives 2.35 µA with all 256 KB kept and 0.97 µA with none). The puck boots normally after an SWD reset, after a reset from nRF Connect Device Manager and after waking from the low-battery System OFF.
- [ ] **I2C on internal pull-ups** (no R5/R6 any more): no I2C errors at 400 kHz over a long ACTIVE run; if in doubt, scope SCL (rise time under 300 ns).
- [ ] **Visible on the charger (0.3.14):** plug in USB-C and leave the puck still for at least 3 min (past IDLE and the 120 s linger): nRF Connect still sees it advertising, STATUS shows ACTIVE (bit 3) clear, and a paired phone can connect and start an update. Unplug it and leave it still: advertising stops about 120 s after STATUS shows IDLE again (unplugging usually wakes it), as before 0.3.14.
- [ ] **Wake counter (0.3.13):** read DIAG (20 B) in nRF Connect. Lift the bow and put it down again without shooting, wait until the puck is IDLE (30 s still) and read again: `motion_wakes` and `wakes_without_shot` are each one higher. Lift it and shoot once, wait for IDLE: `motion_wakes` is one higher, `wakes_without_shot` unchanged. Then over a normal week, note `wakes_without_shot` per day of `uptime_s`: that is the false-wake rate System OFF depends on.
- [ ] **Two-stage idle (0.3.12):** with the debug build, leave the puck still for 20 min: the log shows `IDLE at 1.875 Hz` and the puck stays asleep through the switch (no ACTIVE within the next minutes). Then lift the bow very slowly (about 30° over 10–15 s): it wakes. Repeat with a quick lift and with a slow draw from a bow already raised; note how long after the movement starts ACTIVE appears. With JP1 cut and the release build, the idle current drops by about 3 µA after the switch.
- [ ] **Wake-on-motion (0.3.10, high-pass, default 90 mg = 93.75 mg):** a slow lift from lying flat or from the line wakes the puck, and so does a smooth draw. Set CONFIG `idle_timeout_s` to 5 so that every arrow is shot from sleep: every shot must count, with a capture that starts at the wake. A puck lying still on a desk must stay asleep. If STATUS shows ACTIVE (flags bit 3) again within a few seconds of going idle, the high-pass filter did not settle when the puck went idle (`accel_set_idle`: power-down step plus CTRL9 XL_FASTSETTL_MODE).
- [ ] **Charging:** about 21 mA (from R1 = 47k), and termination at 4.20 V ±0.75 %. Both are within the cell's datasheet (EEMB LIR1255, the same cell: CC/CV to 4.20 V, at most 65 mA; HANDOVER §8 item 1).
- [ ] **Charger LED:** after the pairing window, one blink every 2 s while charging; steady on once the charge has ended (charger done, or the firmware's timed end from 0.3.5); dark within 2 s of unplugging. Through JP1, check the charger still terminates (current falls to 0) and the steady LED draws about 1 mA from the cell; leave it on the charger overnight and note how often it tops up (LED back to blinking).
- [ ] **End of charge (0.3.5):** from a part-charged cell, archesk never shows 100 % while the LED blinks; 100 % and the steady LED arrive together. Note the time from the first STATUS `battery_mv` of 4150 or more to the steady LED (60 min unless the charger terminates first) and the cell voltage on JP1 just after: tells whether `CHARGE_FULL_AFTER_MS` should change.
- [ ] **Charge lift (0.3.5):** with the cell at about 3.8–3.9 V and charging, note STATUS `battery_mv`, unplug and note it again within 10 s. The drop is the charge current's lift (the firmware assumes 40 mV, `CHARGE_LIFT_MV`); the percent should hardly move at the unplug. Send the two values.
- [ ] **Top-up (0.3.5):** left on the charger with the LED steady, no charge current flows through JP1 except the board's own (about 1 mA with the LED, from the cell) until STATUS `battery_mv` falls below 4050; then the LED blinks, archesk shows 99 % and charging, and it returns to steady.
- [ ] **Charge time limit (0.3.5):** with a bench supply at 3.9 V and a 100 Ω resistor across the cell pads instead of the cell (the resistor takes the charger's 21 mA, which a bench supply cannot sink, so the pads stay below 4.20 V and the charger never ends by itself), the charge stops 5 h after it started: LED dark, STATUS VBUS without CHARGING. Unplugging and re-plugging the cable starts a new charge.
- [ ] **CHG_STAT and VBUS flags** in STATUS follow the cable.
- [ ] **Weak supply:** at 4.75 V on the USB-C port, VBUS is detected (pairing window opens, STATUS shows VBUS) and stays detected while charging; measure the final cell voltage after termination (may end slightly below 4.20 V; the MCP73831 is specified from 5.2 V input).
- [ ] **Hot-plug:** scope VIN and VBUS_SNS while plugging a USB-C cable in and out 20 times from a stiff 5 V supply: VIN stays below 7 V and VBUS_SNS below 5.8 V. Use about 200 µs/div and watch VBUS_SNS for a few ms after plug-in, not just the first spike: on rev 0A (R9 fed from VIN) an overshoot is held on C1 and reaches VBUS_SNS after ~0.5 ms. Start at 5.0 V with an ordinary 1 m cable.
- [ ] **LED:** the yellow-green LED (XL-1005SYGC, ~1 mA), at the board's left rim (9 o'clock seen from the cap, 1.5 mm inside the cap wall), is clearly visible through the cap top and side indoors and in daylight shade.
- [ ] **Charge inhibit:** freezer at −5 °C sets CHG_INHIBIT and the charge current drops to about 0. Warm to 20 °C and charging resumes.
- [ ] **No inhibit current without a cable (0.3.12):** with JP1 cut and no cable, cool the puck below 0 °C (freezer): the idle current stays at the room-temperature value (before 0.3.12 it rose by about 30 µA through R3), and P0.11 reads low. Plug the cable in while still cold: charging stops within about 2 s and STATUS shows CHG_INHIBIT.
- [ ] **Low-battery cut-off:** with a bench supply on the cell pads below 3.3 V, the puck enters System OFF (current about 8 µA: BQ29700 4 µA, the IMU's power-down floor about 3 µA, nRF52840 System OFF about 1 µA in high-voltage mode; the earlier "under 1 µA" cannot be reached on rev 0B) after three low readings (0.3.1: about 30 s while active, 3 min while idle; at boot right away). A dip shorter than that must not cut off. Also boot it below 3.3 V after a soft reset while ACTIVE: in System OFF the IMU must be off too (current about 8 µA, not ~0.6 mA). **Attaching the charger must wake it** (nRF52840 VBUS wake from System OFF: verify, it is load-bearing).
- [ ] **Cut-off without an IMU (0.3.9):** boot the puck with the IMU kept from answering (for example with SDA held to GND through 1 kΩ during the boot, or on a board without U2) on a bench supply above 3.3 V: the LED blinks the no-sensor pattern and STATUS updates every 5 s. Turn the supply below 3.3 V: within about 15 s the puck enters System OFF (under 1 µA), and attaching the charger wakes it.

### 2. Assembled puck
- [ ] **Cell protection (PCM):** with a current-limited bench supply in place of the cell, check over-discharge cut-off at about 2.8 V, over-charge cut-off at about 4.28 V, and that a load above about 0.1 A (e.g. 30 Ω across VBAT–GND) trips the over-current protection within about 20 ms. Recovery needs V− to fall back near VSS: with the puck's own load still on, expect it to recover only when the charger is attached; note which. Check the drop across Q2 at 21 mA charging (expected under 30 mV).
- [ ] **USB-C port:** a plain 5 V USB-C charger and a USB-PD charger with a C-to-C cable both start charging (the 5.1k CC pull-downs request 5 V), in both plug orientations; a plug with a large overmold seats fully against the cap flat.
- [ ] **Cell and contacts:** the cell drops freely into the notch onto the Kapton; the + spring presses on the can side and the − strap on the top cap without touching the can edge; with the cap on, the foam clamps the cell and it cannot shift. **No resets from shot shock:** shoot a session and check that the puck never rebooted (a reboot shows as `t_uptime_ms` falling between consecutive EVENTs and `seq` jumping ahead).
- [ ] **Swell space:** with the cap on, a 7.0 mm gauge block in place of the cell fits without load (1.6 mm above the 5.4 mm cell), and the foam pad still clamps a real cell.
- [ ] **Identity:** the puck advertises as `ShotPuck-XXXX`; the DIS serial number (readable without pairing) ends in the same 4 characters and is identical on two different phones.
- [ ] **Pairing window:** attach the charger: the LED blinks once a second for 60 s; unplug within the 60 s and it stops at once, a new phone can no longer pair (0.3.2). Pair from iOS and from Android inside the window (LED blinks twice on success). After the window, a new phone's pairing is rejected and it cannot read COUNT or subscribe; the paired phone still reconnects and works.
- [ ] **FORGET_BONDS** removes all phones; pairing works again only through the charger window.
- [ ] **Re-pair a forgotten phone (0.3.4):** on a paired phone, remove the puck in Android's Bluetooth settings, then add it again in archesk with the charger in: it pairs and syncs. Without the charger the pairing is refused.
- [ ] **Pairing marker (0.3.1):** in nRF Connect, the puck's advertising shows manufacturer data `0xFFFF` / `0x01` from the moment the charger is attached until the 60 s window closes, and not otherwise (also not while it advertises ACTIVE without a charger). With the updated archesk, add the puck: it pairs by itself within a few seconds of the charger going in, on iOS and on Android, without a Pair tap.
- [ ] **Unencrypted links (0.3.1):** connect from nRF Connect without pairing: the DIS and Battery reads work, and the puck disconnects about 30 s after the connect, then advertises again. A paired phone (archesk session, OTA, reconnect after an OTA reset) is never dropped. Pair from nRF Connect inside the window about 20 s after connecting: the link stays up.
- [ ] **Shot log:** shoot 10 shots, reset the puck (SWD or battery), reconnect and `REPLAY(0)`: all 10 accepted shots come back with their original `t_unix_ms`. Rejected candidates from before the reset are gone (RAM only).
- [ ] **Delete a shot (0.3.6):** after the shot log check, write CONTROL `08` + one shot's seq (u32 little-endian) from nRF Connect: COUNT `total` drops by one at once, `REPLAY(0)` returns the other 9, and `GET_CAPTURE` for the deleted seq still returns its capture. Reset the puck: the shot stays deleted and new shots continue the seq. Writing the same seq again changes nothing. Then `RESET_COUNT`, shoot 2 shots, delete one of the 9 old shots: `total` stays at 2; delete one of the 2 new ones: `total` becomes 1.
- [ ] **Lifetime counter (0.3.8):** after updating to 0.3.8, COUNT is 12 bytes and its last 4 (`lifetime`, little-endian) read 0. Shoot 3 shots: `lifetime` 3. Delete one of them: 2; delete a shot from before the update: still 2. Then `RESET_COUNT`, a factory reset from the app (or the pads), an update over the air and a cable re-flash with the Re-flash tab (no `erase`): `lifetime` still 2 after each, while `total` resets as before.
- [ ] **Captures in slot 1 (0.3.8):** with the debug build (RTT log), on a confirmed 0.3.8 the boot log shows `captures: 660 KB incl. slot 1` (408 KB on a trial image until it confirms itself, then 660 KB). In extended mode, store more captures than 408 KB holds (about 20, or 9+ if they don't compress) and fetch the oldest with GET_CAPTURE: still there. Start an update from Device Manager: the log shows `slot 1 handed to the firmware upload` and `captures: 408 KB`, shots keep being captured during the upload, the update itself verifies and boots, and captures stored in the 408 KB area are still there afterwards. After the self-confirm the log shows 660 KB again.
- [ ] **Pairing with the 4 KB heap (0.3.9):** pair a new phone through the charger window, forget it and pair it again, and let a bonded phone reconnect after a puck reboot and after more than 15 min apart: all as on 0.3.8, and DIAG `crash_count` does not move. (The heap now has a fixed 4 KB; its only users are the AES and CMAC contexts of pairing, address resolution and the GATT database hash, 316 bytes per operation.)
- [ ] **Shot path on a puck with history (0.3.9):** with the debug build on a puck that has logged about 900 accepted shots or more since its last factory reset, shoot 200 shots in extended mode: no `FIFO overrun` in the log and every capture arrives. (Up to 0.3.8 each settings save searched the whole settings history: modelled at 45–125 ms per shot on such a puck and 0.2–0.5 s on the shot where the storage frees a sector, against the ~0.17 s the IMU FIFO allows. The lookup cache in 0.3.9 brings the model to under 1 ms and at most about 26 ms.)
- [ ] **Background replay:** trigger `REPLAY(0)` with a full log and shoot during it. No shot is missed and the debug log shows no `FIFO overrun`. The LSM6DSV32X's FIFO leaves the main loop only ~0.17 s after each watermark (half the LSM6DSO32's), so also shoot right after an accepted shot (flash writes) and while a capture streams.
- [ ] **IMU axes:** record a capture with the puck lying cap-up (+Z ≈ +1000 mg), then on its edge in two directions, and note which X/Y direction points along the arrow and which points up the riser. Put the mapping in `docs/PROTOCOL.md` for the app.
- [ ] **Capture transfer:** after a shot, the app receives the header plus 720 samples (standard) within about 3.5 s, or 3840 (extended) within about 15 s; GET_CAPTURE re-sends it; the gyroscope shows the bow rotating forward into the sling.
- [ ] **Update to 0.3.12 from a confirmed 0.3.11 (RAM section 2):** 0.3.11 switched RAM8 sections 2–5 off and the update restarts with a soft reset, so 0.3.12 starts with section 2 off. After the update the puck boots, confirms itself and records an ultra capture without a crash (DIAG reset_cause not 4, crash_count unchanged). Then a soft reset (OTA of the same image or `RESET` from J-Link): it boots again and ultra still works. Let it fall asleep (CONFIG `idle_timeout_s` 5 helps), wake it by lifting the bow and shoot in ultra mode, five times: every capture arrives and DIAG crash_count stays the same (the history RAM is switched off while asleep and on at every wake).
- [ ] **Ultra mode (0.3.12):** write CONFIG with `capture_mode` 2 (0.3.11 refuses it). Hold the bow up for more than 30 s and shoot: the capture has `n_samples` 15,360–15,367 and `trigger_index` 14,400–14,407; before 6 s ahead of the trigger the samples come in runs of 8 equal values, from 6 s to 1 s in runs of 4, then every sample. Lift the bow less than 30 s before a shot: the capture starts at the wake. Shoot 36 arrows in ultra mode without syncing, then sync: all 36 captures are there (about 38 fit in 660 KB).
- [ ] **CAPREC (0.3.12):** subscribe to CAPREC (`…000A…`) instead of CAPTURE and shoot: a 24 B header (type 3, format 2 for ultra) and data packets whose offsets add up to `payload_len`; the CRC-32 check matches and archesk's decoder gives the same samples as CAPTURE does for the same seq (GET_CAPTURE with only CAPTURE subscribed). Time a GET_CAPTURE of an ultra capture both ways: CAPREC in a few seconds, CAPTURE in 25–50 s.
- [ ] **Extended captures at 120 Hz while aiming (0.3.11):** in extended mode, shoot once: the capture arrives in archesk as before (header `n_samples` 3840, `trigger_index` 2880, `odr_hz` 480) and its plots, Steadiness, "Last ¼ second", Pointing view and 3D replay look normal. Before 1 s ahead of the trigger, samples come in runs of 4 equal values; from 1 s before the trigger they change every sample. Shoot a 60-arrow round in extended mode without syncing, then sync: every shot's capture is still there (about 68 fit in 660 KB, 42 on a trial image until it confirms itself). Captures stored before the update still transfer unchanged with GET_CAPTURE. Standard mode and RECORD captures change every sample throughout.
- [ ] **Captures in flash (0.3.0):** after a reset, GET_CAPTURE still returns the last session's captures; switching CONFIG `capture_mode` changes the next capture's `n_samples`; after a full round, count how many captures the puck holds (`n_samples = 0` marks the first one gone) and note the real compression ratio against the synthetic 3.3× / 4.3×. Shooting during a capture write shows no `FIFO overrun` in the debug log.
- [ ] **LED and BLE stalls (0.3.1):** every LED signal looks as the LED report artifact shows it (and as in 0.3.0). Send LED_BLINK and shoot during it: no `FIFO overrun` in the debug log. ### 2b. Firmware update (OTA)
- [ ] **First flash** `firmware/shotpuck_full.hex` by cable: MCUboot starts the app (LED 200 ms, advertises).
- [ ] **OTA upgrade** from a paired phone (nRF Connect Device Manager, *Test and confirm*) to an image with a bumped `VERSION`: upload, reset, swap, reconnect. DIS Firmware Revision shows the new version; `image list` shows it confirmed. Shots, count, config and pairing survived.
- [ ] **0.3.8 → 0.3.9 keeps what is stored (0.3.9):** before updating, note COUNT (`total`, `last_seq`, `lifetime`), run `REPLAY(0)` and fetch five or more captures spread over the stored shots, keeping their seq. After the update is confirmed: COUNT is the same, `REPLAY(0)` returns the same shots with the same fields, CONFIG is unchanged and the phone reconnects without pairing. Of the fetched captures, those that were in the 408 KB area transfer again and are identical; those that were in slot 1 are gone, as with every update (a capture's place in the ring does not follow its age). (0.3.9 computes the stored CRCs with new code; `test_flash_formats.c` pins the result off-device.)
- [ ] **Rollback on reset:** upload in *Test* mode and reset the puck (battery or SWD) within 10 s of the new image starting. It must come back on the previous version.
- [ ] **Rollback from an unconfirmed 0.3.9 to 0.3.8 (0.3.9):** run *Rollback on reset* with 0.3.8 as the previous version and an SWD reset (not a power cycle): 0.3.8 must start. 0.3.9 switches the unused RAM off only once it is confirmed, because that setting survives such a reset and 0.3.8 cannot start with the RAM off (a bus fault at boot). For the same reason, never load 0.3.8 or older onto a puck that ran a confirmed 0.3.9 unless all power (cell and USB) was removed first.
- [ ] **Re-flash by cable over a confirmed 0.3.9 (0.3.9):** run the NAS re-flash page on a puck that has run 0.3.9 for more than a minute with a phone paired (so it is confirmed): `loadfile` ends with O.K. Then once more without the `halt` and `w4 40000984 3C` lines, to learn whether J-Link needs them (if it fails, power-cycle the puck and use the page as written). In both cases the stored data survives as in *0.3.8 → 0.3.9 keeps what is stored*.
- [ ] **Dev page: REG0 switch (0.3.9):** write CONTROL `0A 01` from nRF Connect: STATUS bit 5 goes to 1 at once and the puck keeps running (VDD still 3.0 V on a meter); `0A 00` clears it; `0A 02` fails with ATT 0x13. Leave it at LDO and reset the puck: bit 5 is 0 again (DC/DC). Then the noise comparison: record 10 s still on a table in each mode (below) and compare each axis' standard deviation.
- [ ] **Dev page: recording (0.3.9):** write CONTROL `0B 0A` (10 s) with the puck lying still: an EVENT with `accepted` 0 and `reason` 3 arrives, then a CAPTURE with the same seq, `trigger_index` 0 and 4800 samples. COUNT and `lifetime` do not change and REPLAY(0) from flash does not list it (it comes back with the rejects from RAM until a reboot). Do it once with the puck idle (it wakes and stays awake until the capture is stored) and once while shooting (the shots still count). `0B 00` and `0B 0B` fail with ATT 0x13.

### 3. Shooting: threshold tuning (the key step)
- [ ] Log **every EVENT** (accepted and rejected) and every CAPTURE in the app for at least 3 sessions and 100+ shots, noting any miss or false count by hand.
- [ ] Compare the shots' `peak_hf_mg`, `pre_still_ms` and `post_lf_mg` with the simulated 5th–95th percentile ranges printed by `simulate.py`.
  - If shot peaks sit near or below 6000 mg (light draw weight, heavy riser damping), lower `trig_hf_mg`. The weak-bow simulation set shows recall collapsing if you don't.
  - If `post_lf_mg` of real shots is below 250 mg (firm grip, no sling), lower `post_lf_min_mg`. Watch for more stand-knock false counts.
- [ ] Feed the logged feature vectors back into the simulation, or send them to me, and re-verify before changing the defaults in `shot_detect.c`.
- [ ] Non-shot checks, 20 each: bow set on the stand, knock on the stand, walking with the bow, drop onto grass, let-down.
